01 — GET THE BASICS

Experiment brief

A / THEORY

Aim

To determine the resistance per unit length of a given wire by plotting a graph of potential difference versus current.

Apparatus

Wire of unknown resistance (about 10 Ω), battery eliminator or accumulator (0–3 V), voltmeter (0–5 V), milliammeter (0–500 mA), rheostat, plug key, connecting wires and sand paper.

Principle

At constant physical state, the current through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across it.

V = IR R = ρl / A

For an I–V graph, slope = 1/R. Therefore R = 1/slope, and resistance per unit length = R/l.

Procedure

  1. Clean the ends of connecting wires with sand paper.
  2. Connect battery, key, rheostat and milliammeter in series; connect voltmeter in parallel across the test wire.
  3. Check that both meter pointers are at zero and correct zero error if needed.
  4. Note the range and least count of each meter.
  5. Insert the key and set the rheostat for minimum current.
  6. Note milliammeter and voltmeter readings.
  7. Remove the key, let the wire cool, adjust the rheostat and take another reading.
  8. Repeat until six readings have been recorded.

Precautions

  • Ammeter in series; voltmeter in parallel.
  • Keep the key inserted only while taking a reading.
  • Remove meter zero error before starting.
